How to sit with the timer

Find a seat that keeps your back upright without strain. A cushion, a bench or a chair are all fine; comfort helps you stay. Close your eyes and let the first gong ring all the way out.

Then rest your attention on the rising and falling of the belly. When the mind wanders, and it will, simply note it: “thinking, thinking”. Then come back. No fighting, no judging. That coming back is the practice.

Begin with fifteen or twenty minutes. Sitting every day does more than one heroic hour once a week. When the closing gongs sound, stay a moment longer. Notice how you feel before you get up.

New to the method? Read noting meditation: rising and falling, or try walking meditation between sits. And when you are ready to go deeper, come and sit with us for seven days by the Thames.

Your sits are counted on this device only; nothing is sent anywhere. The gong is a deep, slow Burmese-style gong, like those that call meditators to the hall in Myanmar.
