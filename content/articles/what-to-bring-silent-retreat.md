Packing for a silent retreat is simple, once you know one thing: you'll be outside at dawn, sitting still. Even in June, an English meadow at 5am is cold.

This list is for our retreats at Hardwick, where you camp, but most of it works anywhere.

## Sleeping

- **Tent**, unless you rent one at booking. Tents are single occupancy, for silence and for depth.
- **Warm sleeping bag**, rated for cold nights, plus a **sleeping mat** or camp bed
- **Pillow** and an extra blanket
- **Earplugs and an eye mask.** Birds start early in summer.
- **Head torch**

If you rent a pre-erected tent or a bell tent with a framed bed, check what bedding is included. Bedding is not included by default.

## Meditation

- **Meditation cushion or bench** if you have one you like
- **Yoga mat.** We have a few to borrow if you ask in advance.
- **A blanket or shawl** to wrap around you while sitting. You'll see everyone in the marquee wrapped up like little monks. It's the uniform.

## Clothes

- **Loose, quiet clothes** in layers: leggings or soft trousers, long sleeves, a fleece, a down jacket
- **Hat and fingerless gloves** for early sits
- **Waterproof jacket** and wellies or waterproof shoes for wet grass
- **Slip-on shoes or sandals.** You take shoes off many times a day.
- **Swimwear and towel** for the river and the sauna

Avoid clothes with loud slogans or bright prints. Not a rule, just less to look at.

## Eating

- **Your own cutlery, plate, bowl and cup.** Bring them; it saves waste.
- **A water bottle** and a flask for tea
- Food is provided: three plant-based Ayurvedic meals a day. Try not to bring a stash of snacks for the tent. Notice the craving instead. *Wanting, wanting.* It's good practice.

## Wash and health

- Toiletries (unscented if possible), a quick-dry towel
- Any medicines you need. Tell us about health conditions when you book.
- Sun cream and insect repellent

## What to leave at home

- **Books, journals, music.** Yes, even the journal. Writing keeps the mind in the story. We want to come back to the raw data.
- **Alcohol, drugs and intoxicants.** The retreat is free of anything that clouds the mind.
- **Work.** Set an out-of-office before you come.

## What about my phone?

You switch it off for the week and keep it put away. Before you come, give your family the retreat's contact details in case of a real emergency.

People worry about this more than anything. Then, by day three, they forget the phone exists. It's one of the greatest gifts of the week.

## One more thing to bring

A beginner's mind. Some of you have done many retreats, some none. Either way, try not to come with a plan for what you're going to achieve. Come with an empty bowl.
