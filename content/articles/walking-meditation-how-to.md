Not every meditation tradition uses walking meditation, and it is easy to think of it as a break to stretch your legs. On our retreats, walking is as important as sitting. In fact, most people experience the present moment for the first time during walking, and the first insights often come there.

The Buddha practised it himself. In the places where he spent his rains retreats, there were walking paths. He spoke of five benefits of walking meditation ([AN 5.29](/library/read.html#anguttara-nikaya-an5)): you become fit for travelling, fit for striving in meditation, and healthy; what you eat and drink is properly digested; and the concentration gained while walking lasts long.

## Find your path

Choose a straight path of 10 to 20 steps. Indoors or outside. At Hardwick we walk on the grass in the meadow, barefoot when it's warm.

Stand at one end. Hands lightly together in front or behind. Eyes open, looking at the ground a couple of metres ahead. Not at the birds. Not at other people.

## Stage 1: Left, right

Begin by noting each whole step: *left… right… left… right.* Walk a little slower than normal. Feel the foot as it moves, not the idea of the foot.

## Stage 2: Lifting, placing

Slow down more. Two parts for each step:

- *Lifting*, as the heel and foot rise
- *Placing*, as the foot touches down

Note exactly at the moment it happens. Not before, not after.

## Stage 3: Lifting, moving, placing

Three parts:

- *Lifting*
- *Moving* forward
- *Placing*

This is the main stage we practise on retreat. Later, people add more: *raising*, *lowering*, *touching*, *pressing*, and even the *intending* to lift before the lift.

## Turning

At the end of the path, stop. Note *standing, standing.* Here you can also note *hearing*. Hearing is a wonderful object to snap you back to now. It's always there.

Then turn slowly, noting *intending to turn, turning, turning.* Stand again. Then *intending to walk*, and begin.

## Common questions

**Do I note rising and falling while walking?** No. Rising and falling is for sitting. In walking, the object is the foot.

**What if my mind wanders?** Stop walking. Note *thinking, thinking*, or *wandering*, then continue. Think of a pebble in your shoe: you're not upset to find it. You're glad. You take it out and walk on.

**How long?** On retreat, 45 minutes, then 45 minutes sitting. At home, even 10 or 15 minutes before you sit makes your sitting much better.

## Walking against sleepiness

If you're drowsy, walking is your medicine. Walk a little faster at first, with strong attention, then slow down. Generate energy in the walking, and it carries into the sitting. Several of the five hindrances melt in good walking practice.

## Walking in daily life

You walk every day anyway. In a queue, on a platform, from the car to the office. Note *left, right*. *Lifting, placing.* Waiting time becomes meditation time.

The world walks fast. You can step out of that rhythm, one quiet step at a time.
