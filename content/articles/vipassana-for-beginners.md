Yes, you can. And in some ways a beginner is at an advantage.

At the start of every retreat I ask who is here for the first time, and hands go up all around the marquee. First-timers often do very well, because they arrive without expectations and without other techniques to unlearn. With a clean slate, you simply follow the instructions, and that makes the path straightforward.

Here are the worries I hear most often, and my answers.

## "I've never meditated. Will I be lost?"

No. On the first day we teach sitting meditation from the very beginning. The next morning, walking meditation. Then each day we add one more step. This is how the method has always been passed on in the monasteries of Burma and Thailand: gradually, with a teacher beside you.

The technique itself is beautifully simple. You watch the belly rise and fall and softly note *rising, falling*. When the mind wanders, you note *thinking* and come back. Everything else grows from that.

And you are never on your own with it. You'll have personal interviews with the teacher to ask questions and sort out anything that isn't working.

## "I can't sit cross-legged."

You don't have to. A cushion, a meditation bench or a chair are all fine. Our sittings are 45 minutes, alternating with 45 minutes of walking, and gentle yoga every morning prepares the body. If you get stiff, there is a riverside sauna and a massage therapist on site.

## "I won't be able to stop thinking."

Nobody can. Thinking is the nature of the mind. We're not trying to stop it; we're learning not to sink into it, to wake up from the film and see that it's a film.

You'll drift away many times in a session. Each time you notice and return, you have done one repetition, just as at the gym.

## "What if strong emotions come up?"

They might, and that is not a problem. It is material for practice. You note it, *sadness, sadness* or *fear, fear*, and see it as something that arises, stays for a while and passes. You can always talk it through with the teacher.

If you have a mental health condition, please tell us when you book, so we can talk together about whether a silent retreat is right for you at this time.

## "I'll fall asleep."

Many people do, especially after lunch. It is one of the classic hindrances, sloth and torpor, and there are simple remedies: brisker walking, eyes open, standing for a while, cold water on the face. By the middle of the week, most people have more energy than they've had in years.

## "What if I can't do it?"

You can't fail at this, because there is nothing to win. Some people have a breakthrough on their first retreat, others on their third. It is gradual. Nobody plays Rachmaninoff after two piano lessons, but everyone can begin.

The only real mistake is giving up too early, before you've tasted concentration. So stay for the whole week.

## Is it right for me?

A silent retreat is likely to suit you if you:

- are curious about your own mind
- can follow a simple schedule for a week
- can sit in some position for about half an hour
- are happy to be without your phone

It may not be the right moment if you are in acute crisis or have recently had a serious psychiatric episode. Please talk to us first.

## The best preparation

Sit for a few minutes each day for a couple of weeks before you come. Go to bed a little earlier. Cut down on coffee. That is plenty.

Then come, surrender to the schedule, and let the practice do the work. We'll take care of everything else: the cooking, the tents, the warm drinks and the quiet.
